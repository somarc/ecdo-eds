Evidence — touchpoints, forensics, and the monument program

ECDO’s evidence story is deliberately multi-domain: rock that water marked, shafts that sky marked, azimuths that databases mis-labeled, deserts that remember inundation, and oral histories that sound like displacement rather than rainfall. None of these is a closed courtroom exhibit by itself. Together they are the empirical program the theory says must be allowed under Ockham’s plurality—not buried under “solstice” defaults and Old Kingdom dating reflex.

Exploratory touchpoints. Open to remeasurement and falsification.

Inundation, not “flood”

Cunningham’s public language prefers inundation and oceanic displacement over the word flood. A flood, in ordinary hydrology, is water rising from external infeeds. An ECDO cataclysm is the ocean leaving its basins when the outer rotational body reorients: water that “jumps” and runs across continents for a duration measured in the theory from years to centuries while the system re-equilibrates.

That distinction matters for reading ~175 global sudden-catastrophe traditions. The claim is not that every myth is literal cartography. It is that the plurality of sudden, cold, world-covering water stories is more coherent with a TPW-driven displacement than with local river floods alone—and that mass and anisotropy arguments (see Home) make thin-crust-only models insufficient for that scale.

Seager’s summary of the theory adds a demographic edge: all humans today as descendants of a small survivor set after the last ECDO change on the order of ~7,000 years ago, when much of the world was flooded with cold water. Treat dating and bottleneck genetics as contested research programs, not product slogans.

Pillar three — Khafre waterline and Giza forensics

Among the four pillars Cunningham posts, evidence is personified by the Khafre Pyramid waterline: horizon-disciplined karst erosion marking an inundation line, argued as forensic evidence of cataclysm outside the reach of mere wind or sand. Related claims in the wider corpus include salt and silt in inaccessible internal spaces, reconcretions, erosion-band morphology, and dual-orientation features that register both Polaris positions ~104° apart.

On dating, Cunningham is blunt on X: Khufu and Khafre are far older than Old Kingdom Egypt—“our critical lever.” He resists sinking that lever into “noise, smoke, mirrors, and ego,” which he attributes to archaeology’s institutional habits. Whether or not one accepts the redating, the epistemic point of the lab is clear: if the forensics require a long, stable high-stand of water, the chronology of the stones cannot be treated as a closed footnote.

Pillar four — megalithic azimuths and Np′ obsequience

The prediction/evidence program for monuments is now an interactive database: Cunningham’s ECDO Interactive State and Monument Obsequience Viewer (v1.4.x online as of August 2026 posts) pairs State 1 / State 2 visualization with on the order of 115+ monuments scored for alignment to the dual-state geometry—not merely “pointed roughly north.”

Public claims in this program include:

  • Many pre- and post-transition monuments align to Np′ rather than modern true north or simple solstice targets.
  • Sites repeatedly named across talks and posts: Giza, Nazca, Göbekli Tepe (Pillar 43 interpretations elsewhere in the corpus), Andean complexes, Newgrange, Maltese temples, and others in the growing set.
  • Monuments that would have only seen Sp′ are less disciplined than Np′-aligned sets—read as preference for the African prime point.
  • During the geomagnetic excursion associated with the TPW, geomagnetic north would sit very near Np′, linking field geometry to the architectural program.

The solstice fudge problem

A sharp methodological thread on X in August 2026: academic and popular databases often skew published azimuths toward the nearest solstice. Cunningham’s working rule of thumb: if the solstice lies within about 8° either side of the true architectural ordinal, documentarians declare solstice alignment and publish a false azimuth. Newgrange is a named example—listed near winter-solstice numbers, measured (by him) as Np′. Similar habits are alleged for many Peruvian monuments: measuring from newer (last ~2,000 years) structures to avoid the alignment of ≥2500 BCE cores.

The corrective discipline he advocates: measure yourself; prefer surveyor ordinals and layout studies over noisy satellite “eyeball” lines; next viewer renditions should expose precisely measured azimuths with documentation links. For this lab, that means Evidence is not a gallery of pretty ruins—it is a claim about measurement hygiene.

Queen’s Chamber South Airshaft — star chart over Np′

Inside Khufu, the penultimate stone in the South Airshaft of the Queen’s Chamber is claimed to carry a carved north-sky / star-field geometry over Np′. In interviews and posts this is treated as one of the “dead body” architectural proofs: not metaphor, but an instrumented stone that knew which geographic north the dual-state sky used. Copper-cable and limestone-transparency-to-geomagnetic-monitoring claims appear in the wider TES article set; they are part of the same instrument-building reading of the pyramid rather than a pure tomb reading.

Deserts, ice cores, and geographic memory

Seager’s summary lists inundation evidence in the great deserts and on Giza as expected scars of the last reorientation. Cunningham’s ice-core posts show how State 2 geography is used as a filter: only two Antarctic cores show ~800 ky continuity; under ECDO they “should” because they are not actually equatorial under the dual-state map, State 2 is colder by endothermic core principle, and both sit at snow-capped elevations—while seven other cores lack that continuity. The simulator is offered as a tool for checking such geographic entailments, not only for spinning a pretty globe.

Survivorship and the Euler axes

On culling and regional fate, Cunningham’s short answer: survivability generally tracks distance from the Euler axis points of the TPW rotation—nearer is better. That is kinematic geometry, not moral geography. It also connects Evidence back to States: the same axes that organize stretch marks and seismicity claims organize the human aftermath map in the theory’s narrative.

What would raise or kill confidence

  • Raise: open, remeasurable azimuth datasets with uncertainties; independent waterline petrography; deep OHC gradients consistent with bottom-up heat after advection filters; dual-state models that survive differential revision (see On Modeling ECDO Theory).
  • Kill or wound: systematic azimuth remeasurement that returns ordinary solstice/true-north explanations without fudge; waterline geology that is purely aeolian; heat budgets that fully close without core cascade after honest falsification tests; demonstration that dual monument states are selection bias.

Cunningham’s critical path after elegance is cultural-political, not only geophysical: who also knew; why they did not tell; why the present information environment fights the frame. This lab records that path without turning into a conspiracy feed. Rock and angles first; agency questions second.
