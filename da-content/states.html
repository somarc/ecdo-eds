State 1 and State 2 — dual equilibria of an ECDO planet

ECDO’s mechanical heart is not a free-spinning “pole shift” meme. It is a claim that Earth oscillates between two distinct priorities of rotational equilibrium: one bound by geomagnetism, one released into pure gyroscopic mass geometry. The celestial north star can remain Polaris while the geographic point under that star changes by roughly a hundred and four degrees.

Exploratory hypothesis language — pedagogical geometry, not a forecast product.

State 1 — Geomagnetic priority

State 1 is the world we inhabit and assume is permanent: the outer rotational body (mantle + crust — the “ORB” in theory language) is held in a configuration where geomagnetic moment and mechanical equilibrium share a priority. Geographic north (Np) sits under the Arctic; the night sky’s north is Polaris (epoch-dependent). Magnetic dip poles wander relative to the spin axis, but the shell does not execute a wholesale true polar wander into a second geographic north.

In Cunningham’s phrasing, the geomagnetic field is what is “binding us in State 1 for now.” When that binding is strong enough, the planet does not settle into the pure gyroscopic mass optimum that its triaxial figure would otherwise prefer.

  • Celestial north: Polaris / current north-star geometry
  • Geographic north (Np): Arctic
  • Stabilizer language: geomagnetic priority / magnetic moment dominance
  • Observable today: IERS polar motion (meters), secular mag-pole wander, dipole trends — see Signals

State 2 — Gyroscopic priority

State 2 is the “other equilibrium” the theory says monuments remember. If geomagnetic coupling falls below a threshold—excursion, weakening, permeability and moment language in the talks—the outer body is freer to reorient as a gyroscope. Like a top that puts its dense axis on the equator and its light axis under the right-hand rule, Earth is argued to possess a gyroscopic optimum misaligned with today’s State 1 geography.

In that optimum, a different ground point becomes geographic north: Np′, associated in ECDO talks with the South Africa / Rift / light-mass cusp geometry. Polaris can still be the celestial pole; the ground under it is no longer the Arctic. The angular separation claimed between the two geographic norths is on the order of 104°.

  • Celestial north: still Polaris-class (not a Uranus-style obliquity flip)
  • Geographic north (Np′): SE Africa / Rift-region language in public explanations
  • Stabilizer language: gyroscopic / inertial interchange priority
  • Mass story: triaxial body; LLSVP / geoid differentials read as incomplete if they cannot host the interchange

True polar wander — not nutation, not axial flip

Cunningham is explicit on X that polar motion is the essence of true polar wander in the sense of the mechanism people are only beginning to connect to geomagnetic excursion dynamics—and that polar motion is not the same thing as nutation. In the ECDO stack, the catastrophic transition is a true polar wander (TPW) of the outer rotational body relative to the angular-momentum axis: a reorientation of which geographic point sits under the celestial pole.

What it is not, in theory language: an energy-adding axial obliquity event that tips the planet like Venus or Uranus so that the rotational pole leaves Polaris. Interviews spend time on this confusion because popular “pole shift” media collapses both pictures. ECDO’s dual-state story is a zero-added-energy natural feature of rotating triaxial bodies once magnetic binding is lost—mediated along a least-resistance path.

That path is where Dzhanibekov language enters. The famous intermediate-axis theorem / wing-nut flip is not offered as the root cause. It is the pathway of least resistance the shell takes between State 1 and State 2 once permission is granted by the magnetic/thermal regime. Cause lives in core exotherm and decoupling; path lives in intermediate-axis dynamics.

104°, the 31°E ECDO meridian, and Euler axes

The magnitude of the reorientation in public ECDO explanations is approximately 104 degrees. The preferred plane of the true polar wander is repeatedly tied to the 31st meridian east—the “ECDO Meridian” or 31°E meridional arc. Giza sits on that family of longitude; in several talks Giza is described as near the mid-angle of the 104° arc, and as near the land-mass centroid geometry of Earth—presented as intentional ancient awareness, not as a standalone proof.

Seager’s Uncommon Living summary of the theory, which Cunningham publicly thanked, compresses the cycle language as: geomagnetic cycling shifts the stable geographic axis from Polaris-over-Arctic to the gyroscopic stable axis over southeastern Africa; monuments pre-dating the last change align as if Polaris stood over Africa; dual pyramid shafts register both Polaris positions 104° apart.

Beyond the single meridian, Cunningham posts about Earth “stretch marks”—patterns of disruption and seismicity that appear to organize around ECDO’s proposed true polar wander rotation points, called Euler axes in theory language. The metaphor he uses: hearing a truck in a snowstorm, dismissing it, then finding tire tracks in the driveway. The tracks are the spatial organization of stress and seismicity around those axes.

Np, Np′, Sp′ — and which sky the monuments keep

Np is State 1 geographic north. Np′ is State 2 geographic north. There is a corresponding south geometry (Sp / Sp′) in the full picture. On X, Cunningham notes that monuments which would have only seen Sp′ in the night sky are less disciplined in their alignments than those aligning to Np′—read as a cultural practice that favored the African prime point. He also notes that during a geomagnetic excursion associated with the TPW, the geomagnetic north pole would lie very close to Np′—tightening the link between field geometry and the dual geographic north story.

Inside Khufu, the penultimate stone of the Queen’s Chamber South Airshaft is claimed to show the north sky over Np′—a specific architectural “dead body” claim that belongs in Evidence, but geometrically it is part of the dual-state map: the building is treated as an instrument that knew both skies.

Inertial interchange and the geoid

A 2026 X video post from Cunningham frames the conceptual basis of ECDO’s inertial interchange and resulting TPW against gravitational anomaly and LLSVP mass language. The claim is that presently accepted LLSVP mass differentials and absolute values are incomplete if positive gravitational anomalies principally reflect greater mass concentrations—and that within that corrected reading, the NASA gravitational geoid supports both the ~104° TPW and its occurrence along the 31st meridian, consistent with the inertial interchange model.

This is modeling frontier language, not a closed textbook result. Cunningham’s own modeling essay argues for empirical touchpoints and shared state definitions before black-box simulations—and notes multiple independent modelers now working the dual-state problem. On X he has discussed replacing pedagogical State 2 globe art with empirical State 2 results from modelers, and mentioned LLSVP simulators underway.

Cycle language and speed

Public summaries associated with the theory (including Seager’s) speak of order-of ~6400–6500 year cycling and of transitions that are geologically abrupt—days-scale language in some popularizations, with multi-century ocean adjustment and inundation aftermath. Exact periodicity is not a safe product claim; Cunningham has repeatedly resisted fixed-date Tau predictions even while arguing indigo indicators are active.

State 2 is not simply “hot Earth.” In ice-core discussions on X, Cunningham notes State 2 is colder by an endothermic core principle in the theory’s thermal asymmetry—used to explain why only certain Antarctic cores retain deep continuity while others do not under a dual-state geographic history.

Survivorship geometry in the theory: during a cataclysmic reorientation, regions nearer the Euler axis points fare better; distance from those poles of the rotation is a culling factor in the narrative—not a moral map, a kinematic one.
