Earth may have two rotational states

ECDO theory proposes a cycle between geomagnetic priority (State 1) and a gyroscopic optimum (State 2) — a ~104° true polar wander along a preferred meridian, not an axial tilt like Uranus.

Exploratory hypothesis. Not a prediction of when — or whether — a transition occurs.

What this site is

A calm public capture of Exothermic Core–Mantle Decoupling – Dzhanibekov Oscillation (ECDO) theory, as articulated by Roger B. Cunningham (The Ethical Skeptic) and discussed in recent long-form interviews — including the August 2026 Uncommon Living episode shared by Thomas P. Seager.

This is a hypothesis lab: language, diagrams, and source links. It is not a countdown, not survival advice, and not a substitute for established geophysics.

States

State 1 (geomagnetic priority) vs State 2 (gyroscopic optimum). Np, Np′, 104°, and the 31°E meridian.

Signals

What the theory treats as precursors — magnetic weakening, ocean-first heat, pole wander — and what can be measured independently.

Evidence

Monument alignments, inundation language, flood myths, and chronology claims — framed as exploratory touchpoints.

Instrument

Bridge to Wobblescope: scrub measured polar motion, geomagnetic indices, ocean SST, and more on one timeline.

One sentence

When the core’s magnetic grip weakens, ECDO proposes the outer rotational body can reorient ~104° to a gyroscopic mass optimum — with catastrophic ocean displacement as a historical and future risk — while Polaris remains the celestial north star over a different ground point (Np′).

Epistemic posture

  • Measured — IERS polar motion, IGRF poles, ocean heat products, geomagnetic indices (shown in the instrument).
  • Modeled — field models, reanalyses, pedagogical field lines.
  • Exploratory — ECDO dual-state dynamics, monument→Np′ claims, catastrophe timing.

Do not equate meters-scale polar wander on a modern polhode chart with a hypothesized 104° shell reorientation. Different phenomena, different epistemic class.
